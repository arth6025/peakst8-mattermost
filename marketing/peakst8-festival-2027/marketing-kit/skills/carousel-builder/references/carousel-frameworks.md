# Carousel frameworks

Five slide architectures. Adapted from marketing-skills `social/references/carousel-frameworks.md` (Corey Haines, MIT) and shortened for product carousels made with Sloosh. Pick the framework before writing a slide.

| Content | Framework | Why it earns the swipe |
|---|---|---|
| A list of uses, tips, ingredients | A. Value-Stack | An exact count on the cover is a checkable promise |
| A result with a system behind it | B. Problem-Proof | Slide 1 claims, the last slide proves |
| Several named techniques | C. Hack List | Each slide re-earns the swipe on its own |
| A strong opinion about a habit | D. Rant Callout | Conviction, then fairness |
| A product you can show step by step | E. Demo Walkthrough | Seeing beats describing |

Two rules for every framework: slide 1 competes alone in the feed, so it is designed as a standalone stopper; one visual template for all interior slides.

## A. Value-Stack (4–10)

1. Cover: "[N] [things] for [outcome]". The number must be the real count.
2–N-1. One item per slide, 2–4 sub-points, product visible.
N. Close: one action ("Comment SERUM for the routine", "Link in bio").

Failure: padding to a bigger number. A tight 6 beats a padded 10.

## B. Problem-Proof (6–10)

1. Hook: a specific result as fact, with a number.
2. The real problem, named so the reader recognises themselves.
3–4. The mechanism: named steps, named ingredients or tools.
5–N-1. The detail: the literal routine.
N. Proof: the receipt (a review card, a before/after photo, a chart).

Failure: vague mechanism slides.

## C. Hack List (5–10)

1. Cover: the theme and the count.
2–N-1. One hack each: name it in three words, show it, one line of why.
N. Close with the best hack restated and the CTA.

## D. Rant Callout (5–8)

1. The opinion, stated plainly.
2. What people do instead and why it fails.
3–N-1. What to do, one move per slide.
N. The product as the easier way, without a hard sell.

Keep it fair: one slide that acknowledges when the old way is fine.

## E. Demo Walkthrough (5–8)

1. Cover: the outcome in one line, product in frame.
2–N-1. One step per slide, real product state per step (open, pour, apply, done).
N. Result and CTA.

## Writing per slide

- ≤ 12 words of headline, ≤ 20 words supporting.
- Same type scale on every interior slide.
- Product in at least half the slides; never crop the label.
- Last slide: one action only.

## Sloosh notes
